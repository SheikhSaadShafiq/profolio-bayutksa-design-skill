# Writing the prompts

## The style block

Written once per set, reused word for word at the end of every prompt.

```
Style: natural daylight from a window on the left, soft shadows; shot on a
35mm lens at eye level, shallow depth of field; warm neutral colours with
accents close to <the product's accent colour>; calm and confident; people
are real and unposed, dressed for work, appropriate to <market>; plain, unbranded objects and surfaces with nothing written on them.
```

Fill the brackets from the context card. Change the block only for the
whole set, never for one image.

## One prompt

```
<Subject, doing what>, <in what setting>. <Composition: where the subject
sits in the frame, and where clear space is left for the design's text>.
<Details that matter: materials, time of day, season>. Aspect ratio
<from the slot, e.g. 16:9>. <The style block>.
```

Example:

```
A property agent in her thirties checking a listing on her phone, standing
in the bright entrance hall of a modern villa. She stands in the right third
of the frame, looking at the phone; the left half is a plain light wall,
clear for a headline. Late-morning light, a potted olive tree beside the
door. Aspect ratio 16:9. Style: …
```

## Photographic words that steer well

| want | say |
|---|---|
| a natural, documentary feel | "candid", "unposed", "available light" |
| a product or object | "studio, seamless background, soft box from above" |
| depth | "shallow depth of field", "background softly out of focus" |
| a wide space | "24mm, from a corner of the room" |
| a close portrait | "85mm, head and shoulders" |
| even light for UI backgrounds | "overcast", "diffuse", "no hard shadows" |

## Editing an image

Say what changes, and what stays exactly as it is:

```
Keep the room, the light and the camera angle exactly as they are. Replace
the sofa with a grey three-seat sofa. Change nothing else.
```

One change per edit. Several changes at once drift.

## Consistency across a set

- The same style block, word for word.
- The same camera distance and height for images that sit side by side.
- A reference image, when the model takes one, from the first approved
  result.
- People: describe each recurring person the same way in every prompt, and
  say they are the same person.

## Layouts in two directions, and crops

- In a right-to-left layout the design's text moves to the other side.
  Compose with clear space on both sides, or centred, or write a second
  prompt for the mirrored layout. Never flip a photograph: text, faces and
  hands read wrong.
- Name the focal point and where the phone crop falls ("the subject stays
  inside the centre third"), so the image survives a 4:5 or 1:1 crop.

## Avoid

- Negations ("no people") tend to add the thing named. Describe what is
  there instead: "an empty room".
- Famous names, brands, logos, real addresses.
- Text in the picture.
